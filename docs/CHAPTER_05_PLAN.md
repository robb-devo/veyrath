# Chapter 05 plan: Dragon System

Approved by the owner on 2026-11-04 ("really go absolute full out on dragons. You will most likely
need a bunch of sessions. That's fully alright"). Chapter 04's bosses wait for his play test; their
tuning and the Chapter 04 report follow his notes.

Directive scope: one exceptionally polished dragon: exceptional model, high-quality animations,
summoning and calling, appearance and arrival, landing, mounting and dismounting, ground movement,
flight movement, flight camera, fluid transitions between movement states. Taming, breeding and
more species come later. The no-vanilla rule holds: nothing of it may look or sound like Minecraft.

## The dragon

**Vaelith**, a storm-grey wyvern (wings are her forelimbs, as in the great open-world RPGs): 14
blocks from snout to tail tip, 18-block wingspan, 4 blocks at the shoulder standing. Plated back,
horn crown swept back, membrane wings with torn trailing edges, a tail that ends in a blade fin.
Colour: slate scales shading to pale bone at the belly, wing membranes dusk-blue with veins, glowing
storm-blue eyes and throat when she roars.

## The big technical choice: a real skinned mesh

Minecraft's entity models are boxes on a part tree; a dragon made of boxes reads as Minecraft.
VEYRATH draws its dragon as a **smooth skinned triangle mesh** (thousands of triangles, bone
weights), skinned on the CPU each frame and submitted with `submitCustomGeometry` through a lit,
textured entity render type. The mesh, its skeleton and its texture are **generated by our own
scripts** (`tools/models/build_dragon.py`: tapered spline tubes for body, neck and tail, sculpted head
and horns, finger bones with membrane surfaces, scales and plates painted into a UV atlas), so every
part is ours and can be re-tuned. Animation is a mix of authored keyframe cycles (walk, idle, flap,
glide, bank, land, roar) and procedural layers (look-at on the neck chain, tail and wing-tip
follow-through springs, banking into turns, foot planting).
